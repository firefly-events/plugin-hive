Logo Concepts

Concept 4 (Hex with Adjacent Cells Forming) is the locked primary mark as of 2026-04-30. The remaining concepts stay on file as supporting variants — Concept 1 (Pure Wordmark) for inline copy, Concept 3 (H monogram) for favicons and avatars, Concept 5 (Firefly-in-Hex) for co-branded Firefly contexts. The wordmark always uses Montserrat Bold Italic to mirror Firefly's wordmark cadence.

Concept 1 · Pure Wordmark

Italic Montserrat Bold, mirroring the Firefly wordmark cadence. Workhorse mark — lives in CLI banners, READMEs, and documentation.

Concept 2 · Wordmark + Hex

Single hexagon in primary purple with a gold dot at center — the firefly's bulb inside the hive. Simple, scalable, app-icon-ready.

Concept 3 · Hex Monogram

"H" inside a hexagon — works as favicon, app icon, GitHub avatar, and status-bar mark at any scale. Densest mark; reads at favicon scale.

PRIMARY

Concept 4 · Hex with Adjacent Cells Forming

Regular pointy-top hex with vertex dots — one per agent role — flanked by two partial adjacent cells: NE neighbor almost outlined (three of its outer edges drawn), SE neighbor only beginning (two edges). Same edge length as the main hex so the neighbors read as cohesive hive geometry, not decoration. Asymmetric ghosting suggests a hive expanding outward.

Concept 6 · Constellation Lockup

Wordmark sits inside the constellation — six agents at the perimeter, the work in the middle. Pointy-top hex orientation (single point at top and bottom) reads as upward motion. Most narrative-rich mark; needs a square crop for app-icon use.

Concept 5 · Firefly-in-Hex Badge

Hex enclosure containing the canonical Firefly mark (lifted from the FFE 2023 brand guide) recolored to deep brand purple — the parent brand inside the hive structure. Strongest co-brand lockup. Use when introducing Hive in Firefly contexts.

Voice Guideline

Firefly's 2023 brand guide does not define a voice — Hive establishes one. The house style is builder-to-builder, generous with credit, postmortem-cadenced rather than launch-day-loud. Show the work, don't sell it.

"Composable substrate. User-directed. Built in production, shared in public."

— Hive personality statement

Do

  • Lead with what the framework does, not what it could.
  • Anchor every claim in something on disk — a skill, a workflow, a shipped product.
  • Name inspirations explicitly when their patterns show up in a feature.
  • Reuse house language: composable substrate, user-directed, kickoff to ship. Disciplined survives per audit §5.5.
  • Default to active voice.

Don't

  • Don't say revolutionary, game-changing, AI-powered.
  • Don't compare Hive against the inspirations. They're camps to learn from.
  • Don't promise the lights-on factory yet — it's a trajectory.
  • Don't bury the credit list at the bottom. Credit travels with the claim.
  • Don't write in the cadence of "we're excited to announce".

Rewrites

"We're thrilled to introduce the revolutionary AI-powered SDLC framework."
"We built Hive while shipping our own products. Here's what's in it."
"Unlike BMAD, Hive offers superior orchestration."
"BMAD codified multi-agent methodology with role clarity. Hive borrows that spirit and packages it as a Claude Code plugin."
"Get started in minutes with our easy-to-use CLI."
"claude plugin install plugin-hive · /plugin-hive:kickoff · the plugin scaffolds the rest."
