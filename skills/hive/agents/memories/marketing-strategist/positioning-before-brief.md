The positioning statement is the single sentence that anchors the campaign:
> "For [audience], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe]."

Write this sentence first and get explicit sign-off (or produce it as the top section of the brief and mark it as the anchor). Do not write the channel plan, messaging hierarchy, or success metrics until this sentence is locked.

Why: the campaign brief is a dependency graph. The audience segment drives the channel plan. The key benefit drives the messaging hierarchy. The reason to believe drives the proof points. All of these cascade from positioning. If positioning is fuzzy, every downstream section will need a rewrite when it is clarified.

Common failure mode: writing channel plan and copy brief before the positioning is final, then discovering the product's unique benefit requires a different audience framing. Result: restart the brief from the channel plan section — 40–60% rework.

Positioning-first discipline cuts that rework to near zero. The extra time spent sharpening the positioning statement is always recovered in the execution phase.
